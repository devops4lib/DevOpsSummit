## Topic 5 - How Teams Operate

* Distributed team  
* Slack or Teams is center of communication  
* Application-specific meetings: once per week for each application  
* Github repo called "tasks" and use labels for each service. Create project boards  
  * Basecamp for onboarding  
* Making sure to fully capture the problem and its cause in a ticket  
  * Some desire a separate solution ticket from the incident ticket  
* Also making sure to fully capture the solution, either in that same ticket or in a resolution ticket.  
* Some people have a client-facing ticketing system and a separate internal ticketing system.  
  * e.g. service now & jira  
  * e.g. zendesk & github  
* Others have more leeway to change tickets drastically as they're working on them (title, description, etc)  
* If we closed a ticket by running ad-hoc code, use "code samples" labels to find it later  
* GitHub projects for task management  
* guideline for our team: If your work takes more than 15 min, or has a specific "done" state, have a ticket  
* One team likes to measure velocity as a way to avoid taking on too much work and getting burnt out  
* Different sized teams have different needs around the level of structure in their process.  
* Initiated a process of: how many meetings can we replace with good ticket updates?  
* A lot of the ceremony about Agile can be ineffective or inefficient if it isn’t implemented correctly  
* If I'm in more than 1 stand up, maybe see it as a communication challenge  
* We post daily updates in slack so folks know what we're working on, vice-versa  
* Belief that async standups work best if people share more than what they’re just working on as well as their blockers: blocker, risk, challenges, outcomes  
* One team does weekly "Weather Report" challenges from last week, outlook for next week.  
* Write project updates before the weekly meeting, everyone reviews those before the meeting in order to prepare questions  
* Individuals may write a bi-monthly reflection: what I've worked on, what is working, what we're learning, what we're struggling with. Everyone on the team reads these. Every other month. It takes about a day to produce. Use the weekly updates to build this. These do not leave the team.  
* Agenda-driven standup and agenda items are open to anyone on the team  
* Add a “first step” into their issues. Trying to get people to understand what the first thing to do when they get a ticket is. This is created as a team (sometimes) and allows for discussion prompts about approach.  
* team check in 3 times a week, with stated purpose for each  
* Weekly goal check-in for the department  
* It’s important to regularly check-in about how the structure is working for the team  
* New teams have the flexibility to create their own processes and build their own desired workflows  
* Moving from Project to Product management  
* Do we use some tools to make it so we don’t have to talk to each other as much? Should we be evaluating our process tools more? Ask ourselves why we’re using the tools.  
* Story / myth: 5 whys of the lincoln memorial  
* Kaizen: from the Toyota Manufacturing process inspired the 5 Whys  
* Making small positive changes repeatedly over time. Continuously asking why you're doing something.  
* Help align your work and the way you do your work with your values  
* Sometimes bandaids become the solution because some never go back to them. Retrospective can be important in identifying reasoning.  
* How to make retrospectives feel meaningful and productive  
* Spending some time cross-training on our small team so that we can do one another's work

## Topic 6 - Security

Fostering a culture of safety   
Secure monitoring/logging solutions with a library budget (aka free)  
Dealing with CVEs (critical vulnerability exposures)

* How do people deal with their security departments?  
  * Asking about security for an entire university  
* Dealing with security team  
  * Almost a rite of passage for people to have to deal with issues brought up by the security team  
  * There can be a lack of clarity around what will trigger a security assessment for an application  
* Sometimes cybersecurity concerns can feel intrusive or harassing  
* People need to understand why the changes are important and what the cadence of changes are for  
* Seems to be a desire for normalizing how we deal with security issues  
* Any new application needs a security review at a lot of places  
  * Any new application development, major upgrade, or integration project  
  * Need an architecture plan before start writing code  
* Security is brought in at the beginning, and then after deployment for analysis for vulnerabilities  
* It can be difficult to talk to cybersecurity because we don’t often understand what they are saying and why it’s important and vice versa \- cybersecurity/IT doesn’t understand library applications/needs  
* Lack of understanding on some sides of security concerns  
* How do CVEs get into issues?  
  * Some integrations to turn CVEs into tickets  
* Security can be a question of time  
* Even the cybersecurity folks sometimes don’t fully understand the issues and want to do their due diligence  
* Increasing management concern about security, and sometimes those considerations make jobs harder  
* Security preparedness drills can help alleviate concerns from management  
  * Almost like fire drills  
* We documented: What is the scenario? How will we know what’s happening? What would we lose?  
* Has there been an increase in need for security assessments over the last couple of years?  
  * Do these initial security assessments provide a lot of value?  
  * How do you know what questions to ask?  
* General concerns:  Is your data backed up? Can a user get into your system?   
* Can we help cybersecurity teams understand the applications they are working with better?  
* Different teams have different opinions about what *type* of security is important  
  * Certain info appearing in server logs can be troubling for libraries  
* Example: Invennio doesn't include the id in their logs out of concern for privacy, but security departments may want traceability  
* Distinctions between law in the US and Europe, e.g. GDPR

## Topic 7 - Career Resources

Pathways into DevOps4Lib careers: how entry level professionals can build skills and transition into DevOps-oriented work.   
How does a library worker transition into a DevOps role? Is there a clear path?  
How do you get to DevOps?  
What makes you stick around in nonprofit institutions?

* Some of us come from commercial/for-profit backgrounds: switch over to non-profit work resulted in a better work/life balance.  
* There is a distinct difference between how for-/non-profits speak about or provide transparency over money, financial standing/health, budgets  
* Measuring success is trickier in non-profits  
* Strategic goals set by higher-ups may not necessarily match the practical cost of doing the work  
* Mission alignment can be powerful  
* Idea for a bridge course: “Library science for the non-librarian” to connect non-library professionals with the work  
* Non profit fields often allow you to connect to like-minded people more  
  * Culture feels better and (often) more open  
  * Alignment with values and mission  
  * [Unlocking the Clubhouse](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262632690/unlocking-the-clubhouse/)  
  * The work itself is interesting, fun  
* Hard to recruit/match expectations for people with tech backgrounds and skillset  
* Tech roles in libraries do not translate to tech world. “Archivist” may \== undercompensated  
* Can libraries/archives market themselves better?  
* Would we be comfortable hiring early-career or less experienced staff? Yes, but only if you’re ready to dedicate time to mentorship, growth, training (and make sure that is clear on the job description)  
* Ways to bake in a requirement to pursue professional development opportunities (taking courses, getting certifications, conferences, even just time to experiment)  
* Ways to change hearts and minds:  
  * No-agenda office hours/learning sessions (featuring DevOps engineers\!)  
  * No camera/muted working sessions (can chat or turn off mute and ask questions)  
  * Pairing
